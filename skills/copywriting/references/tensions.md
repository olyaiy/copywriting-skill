# Where the writers disagree, and how to choose

The Copy Book contradicts itself on almost every question of method. That is useful:
these are choices, not laws. The first table records the disagreement. The second is
a way to decide. The "how to choose" guidance is a synthesis; the book itself leaves
these questions open.

## The disagreements

| Question | One camp | The other camp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long or short copy? | Neil French, Tony Brignull: people read as much as the purchase matters to them, if it is interesting. Sean Doyle: less is not always more. | Andy McLeod, Tim Riley, Mike Boles: the best copy is often under ten words, or none. Tim Delaney: shorter is fine, never pad. |
| Is body copy worth the effort? | Brignull: copy is what makes an ad memorable and builds a relationship. | Steve Henry: "don't bother". Dave Trott: it reaches about 5% of 5% of the audience. |
| Whose voice? | French: always your own, a salesman speaking for the client. | Brignull: the company's, as a person. Steve Hayden: the client's best self. |
| Plan or discover? | Adrian Holmes: decide the ending before you begin. Andrew Rutherford: follow eight steps. | David Abbott rarely plans the shape. John Bevins: writing is discovery, led by intuition. |
| Research or instinct? | Abbott, Brignull, Paul Fishlock: dig, interview, observe, read the science. | Indra Sinha: trust your heart. |
| Deadline or incubation? | Holmes: write against the deadline, the fear helps. | Malcolm Gluck: let a piece settle for weeks. |
| Wordplay? | Malcolm Duffy: wordplay, alliteration and surprise are the tools. | Abbott gave up puns for plain words. Holmes: do not over-season. Leon Jaume: skip the closing pun. |
| Entertain or sell? | Steve Henry: always be entertaining; people consume content, not ads. | Fishlock: influence, not entertainment. |
| Alone or together? | Susie Henry: isolation. Sean Doyle: avoid brainstorms and committees. | Abbott kept his office door open. A partner who argues improves the work. |
| Rules or no rules? | Holmes, Rutherford and Fishlock offer numbered rules and proven principles. | Susie Henry: no formula. Dan Wieden: most advice is of little value. Steve Henry: break them. |

The one thing nobody disputes is the reader. Every camp says the copy exists for the
person reading it.

## How to choose

| Decision | Lean this way | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Length | Long | The purchase is considered or expensive, the reader came looking, and you have facts that hold interest. |
| Length | Short | The medium is glanced at, the reader did not ask to see it, or one idea does the whole job. |
| Voice | The brand as a person | There is an established brand voice, or many writers must sound alike. |
| Voice | Your own, or the founder's | The brand is a person, or it has no voice yet and needs one with character. |
| Structure | Planned ending | The copy is an argument that has to arrive somewhere: a page, an email, a script. |
| Structure | Discovery | You are hunting for the idea. Draft freely, then impose the ending afterwards. |
| Wordplay | Use it | The twist is the product truth itself, so the play carries meaning. |
| Wordplay | Plain | The play is decoration, the subject is serious, or it will be translated. |
| Tone | Entertain first | The reader can scroll past and owes you nothing. Then check the product is what they remember. |
| Tone | Sell first | The reader is already at the point of decision and wants reasons. |

When two options both seem right, write both and run the tests in SKILL.md on each.
