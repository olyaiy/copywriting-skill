# Applying the method to modern formats

The Copy Book is about press advertising from the 1960s to the 2000s. Nothing in it
covers short video, landing pages or cold email directly. This file maps its
principles onto those formats. **These mappings are extensions written for this
skill, not content from the book.** The principles they rest on are in
`principles.md`.

The translation that underlies all of them:

| Print ad | Modern equivalent |
| --- | --- |
| Headline | Hook, subject line, hero line, first sentence of a post |
| Picture | First frame, thumbnail, hero image, product shot |
| First line of body copy | Second sentence, subhead, preview text |
| Body copy | The script, the page below the fold, the email body |
| Logo and sign-off | Call to action |

## Short video and reels

- **The hook is the headline, the first frame is the picture.** Together they must
  carry the whole message, because most viewers get no further. Trott's arithmetic
  (few read the headline, far fewer read the copy) applies in the first second or
  two.
- **Do not say what the frame shows.** If the shot shows the product, the line says
  something the shot cannot.
- **One truth per video.** If you cannot state it in one plain sentence, it is two
  videos.
- **Write for the ear.** The read-aloud test is literal here. Short words, spoken
  rhythm, no sentence you would not say out loud to one person.
- **Talk to one viewer.** "Dear Charlie" becomes "you", singular.
- **Decide the ending first.** Know the payoff line before writing the hook, so the
  hook makes a promise the ending keeps.
- **On-screen text is a headline, voiceover is body copy.** Keep the two from
  repeating each other.
- **Overproduce hooks.** Write twenty, keep the one that startles you. Vary the
  angle, not just the wording: confession, fact, flipped register, taking a side.
- **Cut a third of the script.** Then run the removal test line by line.

## Landing pages

- **Hero line plus hero image carry the message alone.** Test it by hiding
  everything else on the page.
- **The subhead does the job of the first line of copy.** It promises that reading
  on will be worth it.
- **Order the page as an argument.** Attention, intrigue, single out the reader,
  show why it is better, prove it with facts, create desire, add credibility, ask.
- **Proof is facts, not adjectives.** Numbers, named customers, specifics of how it
  works.
- **Admit something.** Say who it is not for, or what it does not do. It makes the
  rest credible.
- **One reader per page.** If two different people need persuading, that is two
  pages.
- **The call to action is the close.** Say what happens when they click.

## Sales and cold email

- **The subject line is the headline.** It is the truth told plainly, not a trick.
  A subject that over-promises breaks the deal in the first line.
- **The first sentence matters more than the subject.** It decides whether the rest
  gets read. Start with the reader, not with who you are.
- **Write it as the letter it is.** One person to one person. Count "you" against
  "I" and "we".
- **One fact that proves you did the digging.** Something specific to them.
- **One ask.** Then stop. No summary, no second ask.
- **Removal test, hard.** Most cold emails survive losing half their sentences.

## Social posts

- **The first line is the whole ad** for most readers. Write it last, after you know
  the truth of the post.
- **Find an attitude and hold it.** A list of facts is not a point of view.
- **Take a side.** A position gets a response and a hedge gets nothing.
- **Pay the reader back.** Something useful, surprising or funny in exchange for the
  attention.

## Product pages and app store text

- **Write for the moment of choice.** The reader is comparing and mildly bored. Make
  choosing easy.
- **Facts over adjectives, in the order the reader cares about.**
- **State claims as facts.** Size, speed, count, time saved, with units.
- **Do not caption the screenshot.** The image shows the screen. The words say what
  it means for the reader.

## Taglines and one-liners

- **The truth, told arrestingly, in under ten words.**
- **Ask what the line must do before deciding what it says.**
- **If it is not startling, keep going.** Write many more than you think you need.
- **Say it aloud.** A line that is awkward to speak will not be repeated.

## Critique and rewrite requests

1. Find the one true sentence the copy is trying to say. If there is none, that is
   the main problem and say so.
2. Run the tests from SKILL.md. Report the three biggest failures, with the line
   each one refers to.
3. Rewrite. Keep whatever was already working.
4. Say what you assumed and what facts would improve it further.
